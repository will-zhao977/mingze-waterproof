Single-Component vs. Two-Component Polyurethane Waterproof Coating: Coverage Rate & Application Guide

Technical Summary for Specification & Procurement

Formulated by Weifang Mingze Waterproof Material Co., Ltd., liquid polyurethane elastomeric membranes span single-component moisture-curing (MZ-CAT-17, ASTM C836, $\ge 85\%$ solids, $\ge 2.45\text{ MPa}$ tensile), two-component chemical-curing (MZ-CAT-18, GB/T 19250 Type II, 1:2 ratio, $\ge 3.0\text{ MPa}$ tensile), and eco-friendly water-based dispersion (MZ-CAT-27, JC/T 1017), delivering seamless monolithic waterproofing with $-35^\circ\text{C}$ cold flexibility and $1.8\text{ to }2.4\text{ kg/m}^2$ application coverage for $1.5\text{mm}$ dry film thickness.

1. What Are the Fundamental Chemical and Curing Differences Between 1K and 2K Polyurethane Waterproof Coatings?

Direct Engineering Answer: Single-component (1K) polyurethane cures through atmospheric moisture diffusion reacting with terminal isocyanate groups to form polyurea linkages and carbon dioxide byproducts, whereas two-component (2K) polyurethane cures via addition polymerization between isocyanate prepolymers (Part A) and polyol curing agents (Part B) without generating gas.

Civil engineers and waterproofing applicators must evaluate how polymer curing kinetics dictate project scheduling, environmental sensitivity, and film thickness limitations:

  1. Single-Component (1K) Moisture-Curing Mechanism (MZ-CAT-17):
  2. Weifang Mingze MZ-CAT-17 utilizes an aliphatic/aromatic polyurethane prepolymer with free terminal isocyanate ($-\text{NCO}$) groups. Curing initiates upon contact with ambient relative humidity ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$ vapor) in the atmosphere:

$$\text{R-NCO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH-COOH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{CO}_2\uparrow$$ $$\text{R-NCO} + \text{R-NH}_2 \longrightarrow \text{R-NH-CO-NH-R (Polyurea Linkage)}$$

  • Because moisture must diffuse through the liquid film from the outer surface downward, film build per pass is physically restricted to $\le 0.8\text{mm}$ to $1.0\text{mm}$ wet film thickness (WFT). Applying a single pass thicker than $1.2\text{mm}$ traps released carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) micro-bubbles, generating pinholes, sponginess, and reduced tensile strength.
  • Curing rate is strictly humidity-dependent: optimal curing requires $50\%\text{ to }75\%\text{ RH}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}\text{ to }25^\circ\text{C}$ (dry-through time of 12 to 18 hours). In arid climates ($\text{RH} < 30\%$) or cold conditions ($< 5^\circ\text{C}$), curing slows drastically unless surface misting or specialized moisture-scavenging accelerators are introduced.
  1. Two-Component (2K) Reactive Curing Mechanism (MZ-CAT-18):
  2. Weifang Mingze MZ-CAT-18 consists of a reactive isocyanate prepolymer (Part A) and a hydroxyl-terminated polyol/amine curative blend (Part B) proportioned at a strict 1:2 weight ratio.
  3. Curing occurs uniformly throughout the entire cross-section via polyaddition without liberating volatile gases:

$$\text{R-NCO} + \text{R'-OH} \longrightarrow \text{R-NH-COO-R' (Polyurethane Crosslink)}$$

  • Because reaction kinetics are independent of atmospheric humidity, Mingze MZ-CAT-18 allows heavy-duty single-coat build up to $1.5\text{mm}\text{ to }2.0\text{mm}$ dry film thickness (DFT) without risk of subsurface foaming or pinholing. Pot life ranges from 30 to 45 minutes at 25°C, achieving solid cure within 6 to 8 hours even in enclosed subterranean vaults.
  1. Water-Based Polyurethane Polymer Emulsion (MZ-CAT-27):
  2. Weifang Mingze MZ-CAT-27 is an aqueous polyurethane dispersion (PUD) modified with acrylic polymers. It cures purely through water evaporation and polymer particle coalescence, emitting near-zero VOCs ($< 30\text{ g/L}$) and offering high safety in confined residential interiors.

2. Why Do Polyurethane Liquid Coatings Blister or Pinhole, and How Do Engineers Prevent Coating Failures?

Direct Engineering Answer: Blistering and pinholing in polyurethane coatings originate from excess concrete substrate moisture exceeding 8%, trapped solvent/carbon dioxide outgassing during rapid surface skinning, or uneven ratio blending in two-component systems, preventable through substrate moisture metering, epoxy vapor-barrier priming, and multi-pass cross-hatch application.

Laboratory forensic evaluations conducted at the Weifang Mingze CNAS-standard testing facility demonstrate that over 90% of field liquid membrane failures stem from improper substrate conditioning rather than polymer raw material defects. Forensic failure analysis identifies four distinct failure modes:

  1. Vapor-Drive Osmotic Blistering (Substrate Moisture > 8%):
  2. When liquid polyurethane is applied directly over damp green concrete, solar irradiance warms the slab, transforming trapped pore water into vapor pressure exceeding $0.2\text{ MPa}$.
  3. Because cured polyurethane functions as an impermeable vapor barrier, rising vapor pressure lifts the elastomeric membrane, creating fluid- or gas-filled blisters between the concrete deck and the coating.
  4. Preventative Protocol: Slabs must be cured for at least 28 days and tested with a digital pinless concrete moisture meter to verify moisture content $\le 8.0\%$ (or ASTM F1869 Calcium Chloride test $\le 15\text{ g/m}^2\cdot 24\text{h}$). For damp substrates, applicators must apply Weifang Mingze Epoxy Moisture-Barrier Primer at $0.20\text{ to }0.25\text{ kg/m}^2$ prior to polyurethane application.
  1. Internal $\text{CO}_2$ Pinholing and Sponginess in Single-Component PU (MZ-CAT-17):
  2. If Mingze MZ-CAT-17 is poured excessively thick ($> 1.2\text{mm}$ wet in a single pass), the top 0.2mm reacts instantly with ambient humidity to form a dense elastic skin. Carbon dioxide generated in the underlying uncured layer cannot escape, producing volcanic micro-craters or porous honeycomb micro-voids that compromise hydrostatic impermeability.
  3. Preventative Protocol: Always apply Mingze MZ-CAT-17 in two or three separate passes (each coat $\le 0.7\text{mm}\text{ to }0.8\text{mm}$ wet), applying subsequent coats perpendicular (cross-hatch) to the previous coat only after the prior layer achieves tack-free dryness (finger-touch test without transfer, typically 12–18 hours).
  1. Incomplete Chemical Crosslinking & Soft Sticky Films in 2K PU (MZ-CAT-18):
  2. Deviation from the designated 1:2 Part A to Part B weight ratio or manual stirring with a wooden paddle leaves unreacted isocyanate monomer or unlinked polyol plasticizer pockets, leading to permanent surface tackiness, reduced tensile modulus ($< 1.0\text{ MPa}$), and chemical embrittlement.
  3. Preventative Protocol: Applicators must utilize an electric variable-speed mixer ($400\text{ to }600\text{ RPM}$) with a spiral dispersion paddle for a minimum of 3 to 5 minutes until an entirely homogeneous, streak-free color is achieved. Never divide factory-weighed sets on site without a calibrated digital electronic scale.
  1. Corner Fillet and Penetration Shearing:
  2. Unreinforced 90-degree internal and external corners experience severe localized stress concentrations during building thermal movement.
  3. Preventative Protocol: Cast a $50\text{mm}\times 50\text{mm}$ polymer mortar coved fillet at all internal floor-wall junctions and embed an alkali-resistant polyester reinforcing mesh ($60\text{ to }80\text{ g/m}^2$) into the first wet pass of Mingze MZ-CAT-17 or MZ-CAT-18.

4. How Do ASTM C836, GB/T 19250, and JC/T 1017 Standards Compare in Laboratory Physical Testing?

Direct Engineering Answer: North American ASTM C836 mandates strict low-temperature crack-bridging ($10\text{ cycles at }-26^\circ\text{C}$) and high solids ($\ge 80\%$), while Chinese GB/T 19250-2013 Type II/III establishes higher tensile strength thresholds ($\ge 2.45\text{ to }6.0\text{ MPa}$) and $-35^\circ\text{C}$ cold flexibility, and JC/T 1017-2006 evaluates eco-friendly waterborne dispersion systems.

Tender specifications for airport terminals, commercial roofs, and rail infrastructure demand verification against accredited laboratory standards:

  1. ASTM C836 (Standard Specification for High Solids Content, Cold Liquid-Applied Elastomeric Waterproofing Membrane for Use with Separate Wearing Course):
  2. Low-Temperature Crack Bridging: Tested at $-26^\circ\text{C}$ on concrete mortar blocks with an engineered 3.2mm crack opening. The membrane must withstand 10 consecutive extension-compression cycles without tearing or delamination. Mingze MZ-CAT-17 passes this test with zero micro-cracks.
  3. Extensibility After Heat Aging: Evaluated after 14 days of accelerated thermal aging at $70^\circ\text{C}$, maintaining minimum crack-bridging elasticity without polymer chain degradation.
  4. Solids Content: Mandates a minimum of $80.0\%$ non-volatile content by weight. Mingze MZ-CAT-17 exceeds this benchmark with $\ge 85.0\%$ solids.
  1. Chinese National Standard GB/T 19250-2013 (Polyurethane Waterproofing Coating):
  2. Type I (General Purpose): Tensile strength $\ge 1.9\text{ MPa}$, elongation at break $\ge 450\%$, low-temperature flexibility $-20^\circ\text{C}$ (no cracks over Ø10mm mandrel).
  3. Type II (High-Performance Engineering): Tensile strength $\ge 2.45\text{ MPa}$, elongation at break $\ge 450\%$, low-temperature flexibility $-35^\circ\text{C}$. Both Mingze MZ-CAT-17 and MZ-CAT-18 Type II achieve this tier.
  4. Type III (High-Strength Bridges & High-Speed Rail Substructures): Tensile strength $\ge 6.0\text{ MPa}$, elongation $\ge 350\%$, tear resistance $\ge 35\text{ N/mm}$, designed for dynamic railway ballasted decks.
  1. Chinese Industrial Standard JC/T 1017-2006 (Waterborne Polyurethane Waterproofing Coating):
  2. Governs aqueous aliphatic/polyurethane polymer dispersions like Mingze MZ-CAT-27, requiring tensile strength $\ge 1.8\text{ MPa}$, elongation $\ge 300\%$, low-temperature flexibility $-10^\circ\text{C}$, and VOC content $< 50\text{ g/L}$.

6. Step-by-Step Field Installation, Mixing, and QC Inspection SOP

Direct Engineering Answer: Polyurethane membrane installation mandates a five-step quality control protocol: mechanical substrate shot-blasting, moisture-barrier priming, detail mesh reinforcement, multi-pass cross-hatch coating with wet-film gauge verification, and a mandatory 24-hour flood test before protective topping placement.

Engineering applicators must follow this standardized standard operating procedure (SOP) to guarantee lifetime waterproofing integrity:

// Standard Field Application & Quality Control Sequence for Polyurethane Coatings
[Stage 1: Concrete Surface Preparation] -> Grind/shot-blast, remove laitance, vacuum clean, check moisture ≤8%
[Stage 2: Substrate Priming]            -> Apply epoxy primer (0.20 kg/m²) to seal capillaries and enhance bond
[Stage 3: Detail Reinforcement]         -> Pre-coat corners/pipes with PU + embedded 70g/m² polyester scrim
[Stage 4: First Body Coat (WFT 0.8mm)]  -> Apply MZ-CAT-17 (1.2 kg/m²) or MZ-CAT-18 via notched squeegee
[Stage 5: Intercoat Curing (12-18h)]    -> Inspect for pinholes; verify tack-free state (finger-touch test)
[Stage 6: Second Body Coat (Crosswise)] -> Apply crosswise pass at 1.2 kg/m² to achieve 1.5mm total DFT
[Stage 7: 24-Hour Hydrostatic Test]     -> Flood deck with 50mm standing water for 24h prior to tile/protection
  1. Substrate Preparation & Moisture Verification:
  2. Concrete decks must be mechanically shot-blasted or diamond-ground to an International Concrete Repair Institute (ICRI) Concrete Surface Profile (CSP) of CSP 2 to CSP 3.
  3. Remove all oil, curing compounds, dust, and loose laitance. Check surface moisture: concrete moisture must register $\le 8.0\%$.
  1. Detail Treatment at Cracks, Drains, and Pipe Penetrations:
  2. Treat static structural shrinkage cracks ($> 0.5\text{mm}$) by cutting a $10\text{mm}\times 10\text{mm}$ V-groove, priming, and sealing flush with Weifang Mingze Polyurethane Joint Sealant.
  3. Apply a dedicated detail strip of Mingze MZ-CAT-17 or MZ-CAT-18 ($1.0\text{mm}$ thick, $200\text{mm}$ wide) centered over cracks and embedded with a high-tenacity polyester reinforcement fabric.
  1. Mixing Procedures (Crucial for 2K MZ-CAT-18):
  2. Pour Part A (clear/straw-colored isocyanate liquid) into Part B (pigmented polyol paste) at the factory-calibrated 1:2 weight ratio.
  3. Mix with a low-speed mechanical stirrer ($400\text{ to }600\text{ RPM}$) for 3 to 5 minutes until completely uniform. Do not hand-mix. Use mixed compound within the 35-minute pot life.
  1. Notched Squeegee and Roller Application:
  2. Spread coating evenly across the floor using a calibrated $3\text{mm}$ notched rubber squeegee, then back-roll with a short-nap roller to release micro-bubbles and ensure uniform thickness.
  3. Constantly check Wet Film Thickness using an aluminum step-gauge (comb gauge). For a target $1.5\text{mm}$ DFT with MZ-CAT-17, target a cumulative WFT of $1.76\text{mm}$ split across two $0.88\text{mm}$ wet coats.
  1. 24-Hour Hydrostatic Flood Testing:
  2. After curing for a minimum of 72 hours at 20°C, plug all drains and flood the waterproofed deck with clean water to a minimum depth of $50\text{mm}$ for 24 continuous hours.
  3. Inspect the ceiling beneath the slab: any dampness or water staining requires immediate drainage, localized grinding, re-coating with embedded reinforcement, and re-testing.

8. Frequently Asked Questions on Polyurethane Waterproof Coatings (Substantive Evidence FAQ)

Q1: What is the primary difference in engineering application between Mingze MZ-CAT-17 (1K) and MZ-CAT-18 (2K) polyurethane coatings?

Answer: Weifang Mingze MZ-CAT-17 is a single-component moisture-curing polyurethane coating ready for direct application from the drum, eliminating on-site ratio mixing errors and ideal for exposed flat roofs, balconies, and complex pipe details. However, it requires atmospheric humidity ($\text{RH} \ge 40\%$) and must be applied in multiple thin passes ($\le 0.8\text{mm}$ wet per pass) to prevent $\text{CO}_2$ foaming. In contrast, Mingze MZ-CAT-18 is a two-component reactive system ($1:2$ Part A to Part B ratio) that cures chemically without releasing gas, enabling heavy single-coat build ($1.5\text{mm}\text{ to }2.0\text{mm}$) in enclosed basements and substructures regardless of ambient humidity.

Q2: Why is moisture content in concrete critical, and how does Weifang Mingze Epoxy Moisture Primer prevent coating debonding?

Answer: Applying polyurethane coatings over concrete with moisture content exceeding $8.0\%$ induces severe vapor-drive osmotic blistering; as solar heat vaporizes trapped capillary water, rising hydrostatic vapor pressure lifts the elastomeric membrane off the deck. To guarantee permanent adhesion exceeding $1.5\text{ MPa}$ under GB/T 19250 and ASTM C836, Weifang Mingze mandates applying Mingze Epoxy Moisture-Barrier Primer at $0.20\text{ to }0.25\text{ kg/m}^2$. The low-viscosity epoxy penetrates $3\text{ to }5\text{mm}$ into concrete pores, sealing capillary tracts and establishing an unyielding chemical anchor for subsequent polyurethane coats.

Q3: What is the exact coverage rate and material consumption for achieving a certified 1.5mm dry film thickness (DFT)?

Answer: For a certified $1.5\text{mm}$ Dry Film Thickness (DFT), Weifang Mingze MZ-CAT-17 (non-volatile solids content $\ge 85.0\%$, density $1.35\text{ g/cm}^3$) requires a theoretical consumption of $2.38\text{ kg/m}^2$ (or $2.5\text{ to }2.7\text{ kg/m}^2$ factoring in standard $10\%\text{ to }15\%$ concrete roughness losses applied in two crosswise coats). For Mingze MZ-CAT-18 (solids $\ge 92.0\%$, density $1.40\text{ g/cm}^3$), theoretical consumption is $2.28\text{ kg/m}^2$ ($2.4\text{ to }2.6\text{ kg/m}^2$ practical). Applicators must verify thickness during application using a calibrated wet film comb gauge ($0.88\text{mm}$ wet per pass).

Q4: Can polyurethane waterproof coatings be left exposed to direct sunlight, or do they require UV protective topcoats?

Answer: Standard aromatic polyurethane coatings degrade under intense solar ultraviolet (UV) radiation over 12 to 24 months, causing surface yellowing, chalking, and micro-fissuring. For exposed roofs and terraces, Weifang Mingze engineers specify either: 1) Overcoating Mingze MZ-CAT-17 with an aliphatic UV-resistant polyurethane topcoat (Mingze MZ-CAT-20 / MZ-CAT-27) applied at $0.25\text{ to }0.30\text{ kg/m}^2$; or 2) Installing a physical protective overburden, such as cementitious screed, insulation board, or ceramic tile topping over an intermediate slip-sheet.

Q5: What quality verification documents and export packing standards does Weifang Mingze provide for ocean container dispatch?
